# Lessons

The program's evidence base. Entry fields: **ID**, date, statement, evidence (link), confidence (low / med / high), status (hypothesis / confirmed / refuted).
Selection (`research.md`) and launch (`launch.md`) decisions must consult this file.

## Facts
- **L-000** (2026-09-23): At program start, the owner account has 15 followers and 8 public repos, each with 0–2 stars. There is no existing audience, so reach must come from external channels. Evidence: S000 baseline. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.

- **L-001** (2026-09-24): Hacker News forbids generated or AI-edited text, and Show HN excludes reading material such as lists; it requires something people can try, ideally without signup. So HN posts must be written by the owner, and curated-list projects cannot launch through Show HN. Evidence: news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html and /showhn.html, fetched in S008. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.
- **L-002** (2026-09-24): Reddit is unreachable from the developer's environment through every path tried: the unauthenticated API (HTTP 403, S003), the fetch tool, the built-in browser, and web search (S008). Reddit research, rule checks, and post metrics depend on the owner. Evidence: S003 and S008 logs. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.

- **L-003** (2026-09-24): DEV allows AI-generated articles only with disclosure (the `#ABotWroteThis` tag or in the text). It forbids bot or AI comments on any post, and articles whose main purpose is brand-building or clout. DEV is the only program channel the developer can publish to directly (via the API, with per-article owner approval). Evidence: dev.to/guidelines-for-ai-assisted-articles-on-dev, fetched in S012. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.

- **L-004** (2026-09-25): Since July 2026, GitHub lists a repo's stargazers (with `starred_at`) only to its admins and collaborators: REST returns 404 and GraphQL returns empty edges for anyone else. Star history of any public repo is available from `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/stargazers/history` (API version 2026-03-10): weekly buckets with daily counts, whose totals matched the star counts within 5 % for 500 repos. The program's own repos are unaffected. Evidence: github.blog/changelog/2026-06-30-upcoming-access-restrictions-to-public-api-endpoints-and-ui-views and 2026-09-04-new-api-endpoint-provides-privacy-safe-star-history-data; S016 tests. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.
- **L-005** (2026-09-25): Base rates for repos created 2025-09-25..2026-06-27 that got ≥ 10 stars: 13.7 % reached 100 and 1.7 % reached 1,000. For owners that are users with < 100 followers (counted today): about 10 % and 0.7 %; for all other owners: about 18 % and 2.9 %. The gap is an upper bound, because hits move owners out of the small bucket. A typical cold-start repo with traction ends in the tens of stars. Evidence: `history/research/foundation.md` §4. Confidence: med (26 evenly spaced days, 18,140 repos; follower counts are current, not at launch). Status: confirmed as a measurement.
- **L-006** (2026-09-25): Archetype matters. By normalized lift (share among winners vs among repos with 10–99 stars), agent skills are over-represented about 1.8× and AI tooling 1.25×; non-AI developer tools and playful projects (0.4× each) are under-represented. Estimated conversion from ≥ 10 to ≥ 1,000 stars: skills 3.8 % (3.4–4.2 % across 26 sampled days), AI tooling 2.7 %, curated resources 1.9 %, non-AI tools and playful projects 1.0 %. In the same problem space, the skill form outperforms the tool form: anti-slop linters stay below 40 stars while the humanizer skill has 52k. 22 % of the 3,246 winners mention Claude. Evidence: `foundation.md` §3–4, `earnstar_1/ideas.md`. Confidence: med for the ranking, low for the absolute rates (keyword proxy with 63 % agreement whose errors do not cancel across tiers). Status: the ranking is confirmed; the rates are indicative.
- **L-007** (2026-09-25): Cold starts usually need a burst borrowed from a large venue. The 49 cold-start winners in the sample (owners with < 100 followers, 1k within 90 days) gained a median 260 stars in their first week (12 had fewer than 100), with a median best day of 203. Where the ignition could be traced (7 of 12 checked, and all 7 viral skills dissected), it was one post in a large community (a Habr front page, XDA, Qiita and Zenn, a Chinese aggregator account on X, Reddit, HN) or another person's post, not the owner's audience. HN stories with ≥ 100 points were rare (4 of 49; 5 % of all winners). 30 of 49 had CJK text in the README, which is confounded with smaller follower counts. Evidence: `foundation.md` §5. Confidence: med. Status: confirmed as a measurement.
- **L-008** (2026-09-25): README features do not separate fast from slow winners: media near the top (67 % of fast vs 71 % of slow risers), an install heading (65 % vs 67 %), and a one-line install (55 % vs 47 %) barely differ. Evidence: `foundation.md` §3. Confidence: med (winners only; this does not measure visitor conversion). Status: confirmed as a measurement.

- **L-009** (2026-09-25): The large agent-skill awesome lists cannot ignite a launch. Of five checked on 2026-09-25, three are effectively closed to submissions (ComposioHQ/awesome-claude-skills: 1,382 open PRs and none merged in 90 days; travisvn/awesome-claude-skills and composio-community/awesome-codex-skills: 0 and 1 merged). The two active ones require prior traction: VoltAgent/awesome-agent-skills (96 merges in 90 days) does not accept brand-new skills, and hesreallyhim/awesome-claude-code requires ≥ 14 days of active development or ≥ 100 stars and accepts recommendations only from humans through its web issue form, so the developer cannot submit there; only the owner could. Evidence: GitHub search and each list's CONTRIBUTING.md, S016. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.
- **L-010** (2026-09-25): A DEV article alone rarely moves a repo. Among 687 articles from DEV's top-of-year lists in 24 tags (median 7 reactions) that link a repo owned by the author, the repo gained a median 1 star in the 7 days after publication (p90 12). Of 460 launches on repos with < 50 stars, 8 (1.7 %) gained ≥ 100 stars in 30 days. Reactions barely predict stars (100+ reactions: median 4). Four of the seven largest gains coincided with same-week HN stories. So DEV is a teaching and long-tail channel, not a launch engine. Evidence: `history/research/foundation.md` §6, `foundation/dev.csv`. Confidence: med (no control for how appealing each repo was). Status: confirmed as a measurement.
- **L-011** (2026-09-25): Entering a language's GitHub Trending list can take few stars. - **L-015** (2026-10-01): Typed user messages are a poor source for ranking agent habits by keyword. - **L-021** (2026-10-01): Platform waves are decided in their first one to three weeks. DeepSeek Harness: of about 3,513 plugin repos created since 2026-09-01 (three weeks after launch), 10 passed 100 stars. Jev: the coding-agent winners were set within about 72 hours, and new repos fell 53 % in the second week. Measured the same way (`tools/basecount.mjs`): DSH plugins created in launch week (7,891) reached 10 stars at 10.2 % and 100 at 2.0 % (20 passed 1,000), while those created three weeks later (808) reached 10 at 2.2 % and 100 at 0.12 %, about 17 times lower (partly an age effect: about seven weeks versus four). Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/ideas.md` #27, #33; `candidate-26-27.md`. Confidence: med. Status: confirmed as a measurement.
- **L-022** (2026-10-01): skills.sh (run by Vercel) lists and ranks agent skills automatically from real installs: the `npx skills` CLI sends anonymous install telemetry with repo and skill identifiers for public GitHub repos, and the site shows a "Skills Leaderboard" (all time, "Trending (24h)", "Hot"; about 1.5M installs). - **L-023** (2026-10-01): A model release is an amplifier that small owners can ride without an audience, if they ship within days. Launched zero to four days after a release, JohnHeibel/PDoomVideo (75 followers) gained 1,458 stars in week one, yihui-dev/awesome-opus5-5-videos (97) 1,157, Sahir619/fable-method (24) 1,125, and lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar (25) 716; notable Claude releases came every one to eight weeks in 2026. For a program whose sessions the owner schedules, this needs a session within one to three days of a release and a project prepared in advance. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/ideas.md` (#34 case studies). Base rates including the misses (GitHub search, 2026-10-01): of 408 repos mentioning "opus 5.5" created in its release week, 29 reached 10 stars, 9 reached 100, 5 reached 500, and 3 reached 1,000 (given ≥ 10 stars: 31 % reached 100 and 10 % reached 1,000, against about 10 % and 0.7 % for small owners generally, L-005); the Fable 5 week was weaker (465 repos, 17 with ≥ 10, 2 with ≥ 100). Same-week control (`tools/basecount.mjs`, repos created 2026-09-21..27): topic `agent-skills` 1,404 repos and topic `claude-code` 3,251 reached 10 stars about 3.5 % of the time, and given ≥ 10 reached 500 at 5.5–6 % and 1,000 at 3.6–4 %; the Opus 5.5 riders reached 10 stars at 7.1 % and, given ≥ 10, 500 at 17 % and 1,000 at 10 %, about three times the control at the top. But the Fable 5 week went the other way: its riders (465 repos) reached 10 stars at 3.7 % and, given ≥ 10, 100 at 11.8 %, below that week's control (topic `agent-skills`, 1,386 repos: 7.7 % and 29 %); only fable-method broke out. So riding a release does not reliably beat launching an ordinary skill; it helps when the release itself goes viral (Opus 5.5's code-made videos), and one well-timed repo can still win either way. Confidence: low (two release weeks, mixed). Status: mixed evidence. Status: confirmed as a measurement.

- **L-025** (2026-10-02): In Claude Code's own platform launches (hooks 2025-06-30, plugins and Skills 2025-10-09/16) and MCP's (2024-11-25), no repo from an owner with < 100 followers reached 1,000 stars within 21 days of launch, in any form; the fastest small-owner tools took 60–83 days (ccundo, claude-code-prompt-improver) and MCP servers 153–436 days. Launch-week entries did win far more often than later ones, but their stars mostly came on days 40–160 (CC median days to 1k: lists 40, builders 59, singles 83, packs 104). Fast launch-week winners had audiences of 130–8,800 followers, and the fastest small-owner precedents were tools about the new feature itself (a converter, a loader, a showcase of a full setup). DeepSeek Harness, where small owners reached 1k in days, is the exception and may include inorganic stars. So a launch-week entry is a position, not a burst: plan for directory listings and the second wave. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/launch-forms.md` (235 classified repos, denominators by form). Confidence: med (hand classification; today's follower counts). Status: confirmed as a measurement.
- **L-026** (2026-10-02): The Claude Code ecosystem got about ten times harsher between 2025 and 2026, so 2025 winners are no guide for a 2026 entry. Of 274 repos tagged `claude-code` created 2025-08-07..28, 5.1 % reached 1,000 stars; of the 2026 cohorts, 0.47–0.59 % did (L-024 and the S021 critique). A niche that looked strong in 2025 can be weak now: Claude Code status lines created in that 2025 window reached 1,000 at 6.4 % (no better than the 5.1 % control), but those created 2026-03..06 at 0.12 % (2 of 1,607). The 2025 champion's growth also came mostly after an official docs link and during the platform's own peak, not at launch. Two checks the selection draft skipped and the critique caught: (1) a same-period control for every reference class, and (2) a check of each claimed need against the current release's native settings (here `viewMode: "focus"`, `showTurnDuration`, `timeFormat`). Also search the category in the users' words ("hud", "status bar", "dashboard"), not only the platform's terms: a 28k-star incumbent (jarrodwatts/claude-hud) was missed by "statusline" and "theme" searches. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/critique.md` (ADR-003 section), `candidate-39.md` §10. Confidence: high (re-run by the developer). Status: confirmed.
- **L-027** (2026-10-02): A winner's visible recipe is not its cause. kajisho5/ffmpeg-skill (40 followers) gained 944 stars in week 1; three days later the same author shipped kajisho5/imagemagick-skill with the same one-line pitch, README layout, GIF grid, rebuild script, CI, and `npx` install, during the first repo's peak week, and it has 1 star after 26 days. Small-owner bursts mostly come from unseen sources (`smallwin.md`: 64 % unknown-source spikes, 3 % HN), so copying a winner's form predicts nothing. Method rules: (1) open every repo in a reference class, including the 0–9-star tier, before calling a niche "under-tried" (S021 counted 13 ImageMagick skills without opening the one that was the identical product); (2) check what the winners' own authors tried next; (3) choose reference-class members in advance, not because they contain winners (selection on the outcome turned a 3.5 % class into a claimed 5.9 %). Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/critique.md` (#40 section). Confidence: high (verified by the developer). Status: confirmed.
- **L-028** (2026-10-02): No launch channel open to a small owner transforms the odds; each buys a small chance of a tail event. One Show HN of a GitHub project (5,126 posts, June–September 2026): 3.2 % reach 50 points and 1.46 % reach 100; for a small owner's new repo the expected result is a median of about 3 stars and a mean of about 12, with about 2.8 % chance of ≥ 100 stars in 30 days; titles with MCP, "skill", or Windows never reached 100 points, local-LLM titles did better (13.7× in a 20-post title sample, but about 1.5× when the whole lane of 275 posts was measured; S021, idea #45), and AI-built work meets open hostility. One V2EX "share creation" post (424 posts): the repo's median is 8 stars, +3 in three days, 5.2 % reach ≥ 300. A DEV article: median about 1 star (L-010). Chinese-language repos convert about twice as well as English ones among small owners (0.61 % vs 0.28 % from ≥ 10 to ≥ 1k, same period). So results are decided by a few tail events: plan several well-prepared launch moments per project and accept modest medians. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/scenario-b.md`, `scenario-c.md`. Confidence: med (samples of tens per tier; follower counts are today's). Status: confirmed as a measurement.
- **L-029** (2026-10-02): In S021–S022 every candidate that looked open was already built, usually several times, within weeks of the triggering news: five drafts or leads (#39, #40, #45, Immich rotation, #47) fell to near-identical tiny repos or to a feature inside a large incumbent. Two search habits would have caught each one before scoring: (1) search the outcome the user wants in their own words ("dumber", "drift", "degraded", "nerf"; "image editor"; "benchmark my repo"), not only the mechanism; (2) read the feature lists of the two or three largest adjacent tools (claude-hud already showed the serving model and effort from the transcript). An independent panel that reads only the developer's files inherits the developer's blind spots, so the landscape step must run fresh searches. A consequence for strategy: in 2026 a "dominant open niche" is unlikely to exist for a small owner, so when candidates tie, choose by a stated tie-break rule and invest in execution and launch moments rather than searching indefinitely. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/critique.md` (ADR-003, #40, and ADR-004 sections), `panel-s022.md`. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.
- **L-030** (2026-10-03): Optimizing the selection for star mechanics (base rates, launch channels, platform waves, the forms of past winners) produced about ten candidates in S016–S022 and no confirmable project, and it ended in a question about promotion the owner rejected. - **L-031** (2026-10-03): Behavioural evidence must be counted in independent people or projects, not in search hits. GitHub code search found 180 hand-written "awaiting reply" workflows, but the first 100 hits came from 97 repos of which about 20 were independent projects; the rest were re-uploads of two projects' workflows (not marked as forks). Evidence: S024 log (06:43–06:47), `history/research/earnstar_1/needs.md` ("After the prototypes"). Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.
- **L-032** (2026-10-03): When a need involves AI agents, test it with the models and tool settings the users actually run, not only the newest model. On the same realistic legacy-encoding task, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 with shell access noticed the damage and worked around it, Opus 5.5 limited to the edit tools damaged one file and then stopped, and Haiku 4.5 silently deleted comments, translated strings, and converted line endings while reporting "Done!". Testing only the newest model with a shell would have suggested the need was gone. Also, test fixtures must look like users' files: fixtures named after their encodings (`gbk_crlf.c`) next to an `orig/` folder made the model careful. Evidence: S024 log (06:23–06:36), `history/research/earnstar_1/candidate-A-encoding.md` §2. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.
- **L-033** (2026-10-03): Splitting P0 by groups of people and running one research subagent per group in parallel (each with fresh searches in English and Chinese, quotes with links, independence checks, and an "already met" verdict for each need) covered about 60 needs in about 45 minutes of wall-clock time, and most of them were honestly marked as already met. The developer then verified the strongest claims itself before scoring, and the prototypes, not the scores alone, separated the candidates. Evidence: S024 log (05:55–06:47), `history/research/earnstar_1/s024/`. Confidence: med (one round). Status: confirmed as a method that worked once.
- **L-034** (2026-10-03): S024's first selection draft (ADR-006) repeated the L-017/L-029 failure even under the people-first method: an independent critique found a native fix in one agent (Codex, behind a flag), native support in a 27k-star agent (Kilo), about 25 small tools, and the draft's "novel" repair method already published in the very issue thread the draft quoted six times. Causes: (1) negative claims from a research subagent ("no fix merged", "nothing for Codex") entered the ADR without the developer verifying them; (2) the primary thread was quoted, not read to the end; (3) no Japanese search although Shift_JIS users were named; (4) the prototype was validated only under the fixture's own git settings (`core.autocrlf=false`), not Git for Windows' default. Rules: verify every negative claim the decision rests on; read the main threads in full; run an independent, multilingual, outcome-word landscape audit before drafting the ADR, not only as its critique; test prototypes under the users' common settings. Also: needs whose sufferers look for fixes in a vendor's issue thread are poorly findable without promotion, because the existing tools there got their users from their authors' comments in the thread (claude_encoding_guard: no new star after 2026-08-11); score "people can find it" 2 for such needs. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/critique.md` (ADR-006 section), `s024/critique-ADR-006.md`. Confidence: high. Status: confirmed.
- **L-035** (2026-10-03): Without promotion, the measured channels where new tools from unknown authors routinely get real use are in-app registries, where people search inside the app they already use. At about 60–94 days after listing (entries first published 2026-03..07): Obsidian community plugins by first-time authors, median 293 downloads, 16.9 % reach ≥ 1,000 and 1.6 % ≥ 10,000 (n = 3,337; publication has been an automated, free review since 2026-05-12, about 1,000 new plugins a month); ComfyUI registry 12–19 % reach ≥ 1,000; VS Code Marketplace 1.1 % (about 5 % by six months); Raycast 1–2.6 %; PyPI and npm are not discovery channels (new packages get about 175–230 automated downloads in 90 days); gh extensions and plain GitHub give about 0 stars at the median for unknown authors. Installs convert to stars poorly: about 0.8 stars per 100 installers on Obsidian (median 1 star; ≥ 100 stars in 1.6 %), so the program's measure (stars) will lag the real use such channels bring. Obsidian's in-app search matches only name, author, and description, so the users' own search words must be in them. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/s024/channels.md` (method, cohorts, bot floors). Confidence: med (one snapshot series; downloads include updates and multiple devices). Status: confirmed as a measurement.
- **L-036** (2026-10-03): Twice in one session (ADR-006, ADR-007) the developer scored its leading candidate by a looser standard than the alternatives and then reached for a tie-break the playbook does not contain; both times an independent critic caught it. The scores were written after the developer already favoured a candidate. Rules: (1) fix the scoring standard and the tie-break before scoring, and apply them as written; (2) when the developer's judgement departs from the playbook's result, say so and put the choice to the owner (or change the rule through `playbook/CHANGELOG.md` for future selections, never in the middle of the decision it would settle); (3) before counting a forum request's likes, check its revision history: t/1655's 786 likes were mostly given under an earlier, narrower title ("…in search", retitled 2024-11-25), so demand for the linking job was about 22 people, not hundreds. Evidence: `history/research/earnstar_1/critique.md` (ADR-006 F2, ADR-007 F1 and S1). Confidence: high. Status: confirmed. _S025 update: the owner takes no part in decisions (owner directive), so rule (2) now reads: when the developer's judgement departs from the playbook's result, the developer decides, writes the reasons in the ADR, and the independent critique reviews them._
## Hypotheses (priors to test)
- **H-001** (retired, S013): A Show HN that reaches the front page yields more stars than any other single action.
- **H-002** (retired, S013): Posting on HN and Reddit within the same 48 h raises the chance of reaching GitHub Trending.
- **H-003**: A demo GIF above the fold raises the visitor-to-star conversion. _S016: among winners, fast and slow risers show media near the top equally often (L-008); visitor conversion is still untested._
- **H-004**: Disclosing AI authorship costs goodwill with some readers but earns curiosity with others; the net effect is unknown. On DEV it can be tested by comparing reactions per view. _S016: a caution: "I don't want to read what you didn't write" reached 1,056 HN points (blog.colinbreck.com, 2026-09-21), so AI-written articles must earn attention with data or technique the reader cannot get elsewhere._
- **H-005**: Curated-resource repos earn more stars per work-hour than tools. This must now be weighed against L-001. _S016: estimated conversion to 1,000 stars is 2.0 % for curated resources, 2.8 % for AI tooling, and 1.0 % for non-AI tools (L-006); stars per work-hour are still untested._
- **H-006** (retired, S013): Posting on Tuesday–Thursday at 08:00–10:00 US Eastern maximizes early votes. This is a widely repeated heuristic with no source verified.
- **H-007**: A technical DEV article about how a project was built drives more long-tail stars (through tags and search) than a social post from a new account. _S016: a DEV article alone brings a median of 1 star in its first week (L-010); the long-tail part is still untested._
- **H-008** (S018): A platform launch (e.g., Claude Code "Mods" reaching general availability) creates search demand that GitHub search, topics, and lists capture without social amplification, so a ready, well-named project at launch can grow on discovery alone. Precedent: after the Skills launch (2025-10-16) community lists grew to 15k (travisvn) and 76k (ComposioHQ) stars, after the MCP launch punkpeye/awesome-mcp-servers grew to 96k; their traffic sources are not observable. S018 test (`tools/basecount.mjs`): of 86 repos matching "awesome dsh" created in DeepSeek Harness's launch week (2026-08-13..20), 24 reached 10 stars and 11 reached 100 (12.8 %), against 7.1 % and 1.5 % for that week's 1,728 `agent-skills` repos; but only one passed 1,000 (awesome-dsh-plugin, 17.5k). Second test: of 21 repos matching "awesome skills" created in the Skills launch week (2025-10-16..23), 8 reached 100 and 4 reached 1,000 (19 %), against 24 % and 7.8 % for that week's 180 `claude-code` repos. That control is itself high: in a young ecosystem 7.8 % of tagged repos reached 1,000, against 0.47 % six months later (L-024). Third test: of 7 repos matching "awesome mcp" created in the MCP launch week (2024-11-25..12-02), 3 passed 1,000 stars (43 %), against 4.2 % of all 306 "mcp" repos that week, which is itself about ten times the later base rate (L-024). Launch-week lists outperform in all three launches, and being in a new platform's launch week multiplies everyone's odds. Status: supported (three launches). _S021: weakened. Mods launched on 2026-10-01 with no visible wave on day 0 (38 launch-day repos with 6 stars in total; HN launch posts at 2–3 points; `history/research/earnstar_1/mods-wave.md`), and in Claude Code's own earlier launches small-owner entrants won late, on days 40–160, not through launch-week search (L-025). A launch gives a position, not a burst; re-measure the Mods wave with `tools/wave.mjs` before relying on it._
- **H-009** (S018): Without a social venue, a launch can still ignite through passive discovery if it combines a news hook, proof in the README (benchmark numbers, before/after visuals), one-line install through skill directories (e.g., skills.sh), and a concentrated launch-day spike that reaches a per-language Trending list (L-011).
