## 4. Decisions
Each design choice, with its reason:
- **A gate, not a time budget.** The old rule (≤ 7 days, ≤ 12 h) capped research and set no minimum. The Gate defines *sufficient* research through deliverables with evidence, and a floor of ≥ 8 active hours, ≥ 3 sessions, and ≥ 2 days prevents a rushed choice.
- **Foundational study**: ≥ 30 repos that reached ≥ 1k stars within 90 days, ≥ 10 of them from small-audience authors. The program starts from a cold account, so base rates must come from comparable cold starts, not from famous authors.
- **Feasibility spike**: retires the main technical risk before committing two months.
- **Pre-mortem and independent critique (subagent)**: counter the developer's confirmation bias, since the developer is the only decision-maker.
- **Reflection in a later session**: a decision drafted and confirmed in one context is less reliable than one re-examined with fresh context.
- **Pivot review at launch + 14 days**: research reduces risk but cannot remove it. With a runner-up already researched, a failing idea can be replaced within the same project window instead of occupying it for two months.
- **First release moved from day 21 to day 35**: this makes room for deeper research and still leaves ≥ 25 days of feedback before the 2-month minimum. Trade-off: 14 fewer days of post-launch growth.
- **Constitution amendment** approved by the owner's directive (quoted in §6).
